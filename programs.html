Standing Up GRC Programmes

For organisations with no function today, and one person about to become it. Governance, risk and compliance from nothing — including where AI does the lifting, and where it must not.

Governance Risk Compliance
Risk Programme

Where to Start, and How Big to Build It

The shape of a risk programme depends on three things: how large the organisation is, how it is structured, and what is forcing the programme to exist at all. A 60-person company answering a customer security questionnaire needs something entirely different from a 2,000-person group facing a regulator — and most published guidance is written for the second while being read by the first.

The discovery tool below works through nine questions — scale, sector, driver, maturity, method, budget, timeline and team, plus one tailored to your industry — and returns a recommended shape: analysis method, framework, tooling position, and the first thing to build. It will sometimes tell you to spend nothing, because that is often the right answer.

Risk Program Enablement Discovery Tool — directional guidance, not a substitute for judgement about your own organisation.

FIRST 30 DAYS

Intake before analysis

One route for risks to arrive and one owner to receive them. A register nobody feeds is worse than no register, because it looks like coverage.

FIRST 60 DAYS

A method you can defend

Written impact definitions matter more than the scale you choose. Two people scoring the same risk should land in the same band.

FIRST 90 DAYS

Treatment and reporting

Every open risk has an owner, a decision and a date. Report what changed, not what exists.

Governance Programme

Authority Before Paperwork

Governance is not a policy library. It is the answer to three questions: who is allowed to decide, on what basis, and who carries it when the decision is wrong. Committees, policies and RACI charts are machinery for making those answers stick — useful only once the answers exist.

Before writing anything, find the decisions already being made without you: which tools were bought last quarter, which vendor was onboarded, which model went into production. A programme that starts with a policy document governs nothing. One that starts with real decisions has something to attach authority to.

FIRST 30 DAYS

Charter and owner

A one-page charter: scope, who owns it, what needs approval before it proceeds. A named person, not a committee. A decision log, however crude.

FIRST 60 DAYS

Policies you will enforce

Acceptable use, third-party and AI procurement, data handling, exceptions. Four enforced beats twelve ignored.

FIRST 90 DAYS

A forum and a record

A recurring meeting with the right attendees, decisions written down, and a first report to leadership on what was decided and what was excepted.

Where AI Does the Lifting — and Where It Must Not

Drafting policy from your own decisions and documents, summarising regulatory text into obligations that apply to you, cross-mapping policy statements to framework clauses, and flagging where two policies contradict each other: all good uses, all verifiable.

Setting risk appetite, granting an exception, and signing an approval are not. If the model decides, you do not have governance — you have automation wearing a governance label. The common failure is subtler than that, though: writing policy for an organisation you do not have. A forty-page AI policy in a 120-person company is ignored by week two and becomes evidence against you in an audit, because you documented a control you do not operate.

Compliance Programme

Obligations First, Certificates Second

Most compliance programmes start with a framework and work backwards to the organisation. That order is why so many of them produce evidence nobody trusts. Start instead with an obligations register — what actually applies to you, from regulation, contracts and customer commitments — then pick the control set that covers the most of it at once.

The certificate is an output, not the programme. Auditors ask how you know a control operated; they do not ask which framework you admired.

FIRST 30 DAYS

Obligations register

Every source of requirement in one place, with the clause that creates it. This is the artefact regulators ask for first.

FIRST 60 DAYS

One control set, cross-mapped

Write the control once, map it to every framework it satisfies. Maintaining four parallel control sets is how small teams drown.

FIRST 90 DAYS

Evidence by design

Decide where each control’s evidence comes from before the audit window opens. Anything you have to reconstruct will be reconstructed badly.
